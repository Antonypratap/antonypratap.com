A website revamp is an opportunity to fix what is not working. It is also an easy way to lose search rankings, break enquiry forms and spend months on design debates. The difference is usually made before any design work starts.

This checklist covers the decisions to make first. It suits service businesses of any size, from logistics and trades to professional services.

## Purpose and audience

### 1. What should the website achieve?

Pick one or two measurable goals: more quote requests, more phone calls, fewer "where is my order?" emails, more job applications. Every later decision should serve those goals.

### 2. Who visits, and what do they need?

List your main visitor types (new customers, existing customers, suppliers, job seekers) and the one thing each needs to do. A logistics customer may want a quote or to track a shipment. A job seeker wants vacancies.

### 3. What is wrong with the current site?

Ask customers and staff what is confusing, slow or missing. Check which pages actually get visits and which forms generate enquiries. Keep what works.

## Content

### 4. Which pages stay, merge or go?

Make a list of every existing page and decide its future. Pages that rank in search or bring enquiries should be kept or improved, not deleted.

### 5. Who writes the content, and by when?

Content is the most common cause of delay. Assign owners and deadlines early, and plan the content before the design is finalised.

## Platform

### 6. Which platform suits how you will run the site?

A hosted platform such as Framer suits a polished, low-maintenance marketing site. WordPress suits sites that need ecommerce, complex content or integrations. See [Framer or WordPress?](/insights/framer-vs-wordpress-business-website/) for the trade-offs.

### 7. Who will maintain it?

Decide who updates content and who handles technical maintenance after launch. A site nobody owns slowly decays.

## Search engines

### 8. Protect your existing rankings

Before launch, list every current URL and decide where it will go. Set up **permanent (301) redirects** from old URLs to their new equivalents. Skipping this step is the most common reason a revamped site loses search traffic.

### 9. Build in the basics

Unique page titles and descriptions, a clear heading structure, fast loading, mobile-friendly layouts, an XML sitemap and a connection to Google Search Console.

## Forms and integrations

### 10. Where do enquiries go?

Quote forms, contact forms and bookings should reach the right person or system reliably, with a copy kept. Test them from different devices before launch.

### 11. What should connect now, and what later?

The website may eventually need to connect to a CRM, booking system, tracking system or accounting software. You do not have to build everything at launch, but choose a platform that will not block what you need next.

## Launch

### 12. Plan the launch, then measure

Test on phones and desktops, check every redirect, confirm analytics and forms work, and launch at a quiet time. Afterwards, compare the results against the goals you set in step 1.

A revamp does not have to be one big launch. Starting with the pages and features that matter most, then improving in stages, gets the benefits sooner and lowers the risk. If you are planning a revamp, see [technology consulting and implementation](/services/technology-consulting/).
