Choosing a website platform looks like a design decision. It is really an operating decision. It decides who can update the site, what it costs to run, how much maintenance it needs and what it can connect to later.

Framer and WordPress come up in almost every conversation about a new business website. They suit different businesses. This guide sets out the differences that matter and a simple way to decide.

## The short answer

- **Choose Framer** when the website's job is to present the business well: a polished, fast marketing site that the team can update without a developer, with little or no maintenance.
- **Choose WordPress** when the website is part of how the business operates: online ordering, ecommerce with WooCommerce, member areas, complex content, or integrations with inventory, accounting and payment systems.

Most of the rest of this article explains why.

## What each platform is

**Framer** is a hosted website builder. You design and publish in Framer, and Framer runs the hosting, security and performance. There are no servers or plugins to manage.

**WordPress** is open-source software that you install on hosting you choose. Its strength is its ecosystem: thousands of themes and plugins, WooCommerce for ecommerce, and developers in every market. The trade-off is that you, or someone you pay, are responsible for hosting, updates, security and plugin compatibility.

## Comparison

| | Framer | WordPress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting and security | Included and managed | Your responsibility (or your host's) |
| Maintenance | Very little | Regular core, theme and plugin updates |
| Design control | High, visual and precise | Depends on theme and builder |
| Editing by non-technical staff | Straightforward for content and pages | Straightforward once set up well |
| Ecommerce | Limited | Mature, through WooCommerce |
| Integrations with business systems | Limited to what the platform supports | Extensive, through plugins and custom code |
| Content at scale | Good for marketing content | Strong for large blogs and complex content |
| Moving away later | The site has to be rebuilt elsewhere | Content and code can move between hosts |

## Questions that decide it

### What is the website for?

If the site exists to explain what you do, show your work and generate enquiries, Framer's speed and low maintenance are hard to beat. If customers will order, pay, log in or check stock on the site, you need the depth of WordPress (or a dedicated ecommerce platform).

### Who will look after it?

A WordPress site needs ongoing care: updates, backups, security monitoring and occasional fixes when a plugin update breaks something. If nobody owns that, a hosted platform is the safer choice.

### What does it need to connect to?

This is the question most often missed. A website that has to exchange data with an ERP, accounting software, inventory system or payment gateway needs a platform that supports those integrations properly. WordPress, with WooCommerce and its plugin ecosystem, is usually the stronger option here. A good example is a distribution business whose website has to work with its accounting and payment systems.

### How much content will you publish?

Both handle a company blog. For a large content operation with many authors, categories and custom content types, WordPress gives more room to grow.

### What will it cost over three years?

Compare the full cost, not the launch price. With Framer, that is mainly the subscription. With WordPress, add hosting, premium plugins, maintenance time and the cost of fixing problems. A cheap WordPress launch can become the more expensive option once maintenance is included.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing on looks alone.** Both can look excellent. Choose on what the site has to do and who will run it.
- **Ignoring maintenance.** An unmaintained WordPress site is a security risk.
- **Forgetting future integrations.** If online ordering or system integration is likely within a year or two, plan for it now.
- **Underestimating lock-in.** Leaving a hosted builder usually means rebuilding the site. That is acceptable for a marketing site, but worth knowing in advance.

## A simple decision rule

If the website is a **shop window**, Framer is usually the better choice. If the website is **part of the operation**, start with WordPress or an ecommerce platform, and plan the integrations from day one.
