Under the UAE's e-invoicing model, businesses exchange invoices through **Accredited Service Providers (ASPs)**, companies accredited by the Ministry of Finance to connect to the Peppol-based network. Choosing an ASP is therefore one of the first decisions in an e-invoicing project, and one of the hardest to reverse.

Most ASPs will meet the regulatory requirements. They differ in how well they fit your systems, how much work they leave for your team and how they handle problems. These ten questions help you compare them. For the wider picture, read [UAE e-invoicing: what has to change in your ERP and accounting systems](/insights/uae-e-invoicing-erp-accounting-systems/).

> Confirm that any provider appears on the Ministry of Finance's current list of accredited providers, and check current requirements and deadlines on official government websites.

## About your systems

### 1. Do you connect to our ERP or accounting system today?

Ask for a working connector for your exact software and version, not a roadmap item. Ask to speak to a customer using the same system.

### 2. How does invoice data get from our system to you?

A native connector, an API, file exchange or manual upload? Each has different implications for automation, error handling and IT effort.

### 3. Can you handle all our invoicing sources?

Many businesses invoice from more than one place: an ERP, a separate billing tool and occasional manual invoices. Each needs a route to the ASP.

### 4. How do you handle several legal entities?

Groups with mainland and free zone entities, or several companies, should check how each entity is set up, billed and managed.

## About onboarding and operations

### 5. What does onboarding involve, and how long does it take?

Ask for a realistic plan with your team's tasks, not only the provider's. Data mapping and testing usually take longer than the connection itself.

### 6. How do we receive invoices from suppliers?

E-invoicing covers incoming invoices too. Ask how received invoices reach your system, in what format, and whether they can feed automated matching with purchase orders and goods receipts.

### 7. What happens when an invoice is rejected?

Ask to see the error messages your team will receive, where they appear and how corrected invoices are resent. Clear, actionable errors save hours every week.

### 8. What support do you provide, and when?

Support hours, languages, response times and escalation. Invoicing problems at month end cannot wait for a ticket queue.

## About cost and commitment

### 9. How is it priced?

Per invoice, per entity, by subscription or a mix? Model the cost on your real annual volumes of sent and received invoices, including credit notes.

### 10. What happens if we want to leave?

Ask about contract length, data export and how switching to another ASP works. A provider that makes leaving easy is usually confident in its service.

## Making the decision

Shortlist two or three providers, ask each the same questions in writing and test the connector with real invoices from your system before signing.
